Five Navy bombers took off for a routine training flight. None of them ever came back.

December 1945. Flight 19 left Florida with fourteen men aboard. Two hours in, the flight leader radioed that both of his compasses had failed.

"We cannot tell where we are," he said. "Everything looks strange. Even the ocean."

For four hours, controllers listened as the planes circled, lost. The last words: "It looks like we are entering white water." Then static.

A rescue plane launched with thirteen men aboard. Twenty minutes later, it vanished too.

Six aircraft. Twenty-seven men. One afternoon. The Navy's investigation filled five hundred pages — and closed with two words: cause unknown.

These are the same waters that swallowed the USS Cyclops and her three hundred and nine men.

But the strangest disappearance here was not planes or ships. It was an entire merchant crew — the ship found sailing on, perfectly intact, with nobody aboard.
