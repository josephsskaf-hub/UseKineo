## 2. SCRIPT (207 palavras · ~64s)

```
In 2012, one image on 4chan started hunting for geniuses.

It was plain black and white. The text said they were looking for highly
intelligent individuals. It was signed with a number: 3301.

Most people scrolled past. The ones who didn't found something strange. The
answer wasn't in the picture. It was hidden inside the file itself.

That unlocked another layer. Then a cipher. Then a phone number that actually
worked, with a recorded voice giving out more clues.

And then it left the internet entirely. The trail pointed to GPS coordinates in
fourteen cities across the world. Real posters, taped to real street poles, in
Warsaw, in Seoul, in Sydney. Each one carried a code.

Whoever 3301 was, they had people on the ground on multiple continents.

The puzzle came back in 2013. Then again in 2014. And that year they released
the last piece: a book called Liber Primus, written entirely in runes. Somewhere
between fifty-eight and seventy-four pages.

Then 3301 went silent. No message, no explanation, nothing since.

Here's the part that keeps people up at night. It's 2026, and almost all of
Liber Primus is still unreadable. Twelve years of the smartest people online,
and the book won't open.

But this isn't the strangest thing the internet ever posted. One account uploaded
a coded block every single day, for years, and never once said why. Follow —
that one's next.
```
